# Taking reading mode to deaf community organisations

A kit for roadmap product item 6. Nothing here has been sent. The owner
decides who to contact and sends every message personally.

## What we are offering

Mindline is live typing between browsers: each person sees the other's
words as they are typed, and the text dries into a sent message. There is
no account and no app to install; a room is a link. Messages travel device
to device, end-to-end encrypted, and nothing is kept on a server.

Reading mode (the "Aa" button, in the start page and room headers) has four
settings, kept on the device:

- Text size: default, large (112.5%), extra large (125%). The whole screen
  scales together.
- High contrast: body text at 16.1:1 in light mode and 14.5:1 in dark.
- Show drafts steadily: the other person's forming text never fades, pulses,
  or dims.
- Read drafts aloud: a screen reader speaks the other person's new words a
  phrase at a time, never per keystroke.

Settings apply before the first paint, so returning readers never see a
flash of small text.

## What it is not

Say this plainly in every conversation:

- Not a relay service, and not connected to phone RTT, TTY, or emergency
  numbers. It cannot call 911, 112, or 999.
- Not certified against any accessibility or telecom standard.
- Built for small rooms (about 8 people), in a web browser.
- Security relies on the invite link: anyone who has it can ask to join.

## Who to approach

Start with organisations that already run programmes on text communication
or technology access. Check each one's current contact route before writing;
this list names candidates, not confirmed contacts.

- National associations of deaf people (for example the National
  Association of the Deaf in the US, the Deutscher Gehörlosen-Bund in
  Germany), usually through a technology or accessibility committee.
- Hearing loss organisations (for example the Hearing Loss Association of
  America, RNID in the UK), which reach late-deafened and hard-of-hearing
  people who rely on captions and text.
- The World Federation of the Deaf, for a view across countries.
- University accessibility offices and deaf studies departments, which can
  arrange a small user session.
- Local deaf clubs and community centres, for hands-on feedback.

> Subject: Live-typing chat with a reading mode, looking for feedback
>
> Hello [name],
>
> I build Mindline (https://mindline.chat), a free web chat where you see
> the other person's words as they type them, like real-time text. There
> is no account or app: a conversation is a link.
>
> I added a reading mode for people who read conversations as text all
> day: larger text, high contrast, drafts that stay steady instead of
> fading, and an option for screen readers to speak the other person's
> words a phrase at a time.
>
> I would like to learn whether this is useful to your members, and what
> is missing or wrong. It is not a relay service and does not connect to
> phone RTT or emergency numbers.
>
> Would you or a colleague try it for fifteen minutes, or point me to
> someone who runs user sessions? I can join by video with captions, or
> we can do the whole session in Mindline itself.
>
> Thank you,
> [your name]

## A fifteen-minute session

1. Send the person a room link. They open it on their own phone or laptop.
2. Ask them to open "Aa" and choose the settings they would use every day.
3. Hold a normal conversation for five minutes. Type naturally, including
   corrections, so they see drafts change.
4. Turn on "Hold drafts" for a message, so they see the difference between
   live and finished text.
5. Ask the questions below. Write down their words, not your summary.

## Questions to ask

- Could you follow the conversation comfortably? What made it harder?
- Which reading settings did you choose, and why?
- Is seeing the other person type helpful, tiring, or both? When?
- Would you use this instead of, or next to, what you use now? For what?
- What would stop you from recommending it to someone else?
- Is there anything in the wording, colours, or layout that confused you?

## After each session

Record the date, the organisation, the settings chosen, and the answers in
an issue on the repository (without names unless the person agrees). Fix
what repeats across two or more sessions before the next round of contacts.
